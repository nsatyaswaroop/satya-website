About

My career didn’t begin in the cloud. It began with curiosity — the kind that makes you take apart systems just to understand how they breathe. Over the last decade, that curiosity has taken me from legacy insurance platforms to modern Snowflake architectures, from hand‑built SQL models to AI‑assisted engineering workflows, and from traditional BI reporting to intelligent, automated analytics ecosystems.

I’ve spent my life inside data systems — building them, modernizing them, and sometimes rescuing them. I’ve led teams through migrations that felt impossible, designed ingestion frameworks that became the backbone of enterprise analytics, and used AI not as a buzzword but as a practical engineering tool.

Today, I focus on the intersection of cloud engineering, Snowflake ingestion, PostgreSQL optimization, and AI‑driven automation. My work is about clarity: making data systems faster, cleaner, more predictable, and more human‑friendly.

Career Journey

My career has been a steady climb from BI engineer to cloud architect — not through titles, but through solving harder and harder problems. I’ve worked across insurance, financial services, manufacturing, and global operations, but the theme has always been the same: take something complex, outdated, or fragile… and rebuild it into something modern, scalable, and elegant.

Experian Services — Team Lead

At Experian, I stepped into a modernization effort that touched every part of the data ecosystem. Legacy DataStage pipelines were slowing down innovation, so I rebuilt them using AWS Glue, Snowflake ingestion patterns, and PostgreSQL components designed for long‑term scalability. I introduced Open Spec Development to bring consistency to engineering practices and used Generative AI to accelerate documentation, SQL tuning, and pipeline validation.

Germania Insurance — BI Lead / Data Engineer

Germania was where I learned to build systems that last. Over seven years, I migrated legacy platforms into Snowflake, designed Spark + dbt ingestion pipelines, and delivered dashboards that reshaped underwriting and claims analytics. I built models that actuaries trusted, pipelines that engineers relied on, and BI layers that executives used to make real decisions.

Earlier Roles

Before that, I worked across ThermoFisher, General Motors, Deloitte, and Accenture — building Cognos frameworks, migrating reporting systems, and learning how enterprise data behaves at scale. These roles gave me the foundation I still use today: dimensional modeling, warehouse design, and the discipline of building systems that don’t break under pressure.

Engineering Philosophy

I believe great data systems are quiet. They don’t draw attention to themselves. They run predictably, scale gracefully, and make life easier for the people who depend on them. My job is to build those systems — the ones that disappear into the background because they simply work.

I also believe AI belongs inside engineering, not outside it. Not as a replacement for human judgment, but as a tool that amplifies it. AI can validate pipelines, generate documentation, detect anomalies, and accelerate development — but only when guided by engineers who understand the craft.

At the end of the day, I’m not trying to build the flashiest systems. I’m trying to build the ones that last.
